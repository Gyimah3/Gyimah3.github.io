Guidance, Not Adaptation: What Makes an Open Text-to-Music Model Sing isiZulu

1st Africa in AI Workshop, NeurIPS 2026
Springer LNCS · accepted October 2026

Open text-to-music models advertise support for fifty or more languages. To our knowledge, none had been evaluated on an African one. I adapted ACE-Step 1.5, a 2B-parameter flow-matching diffusion transformer, to isiZulu across ten genre families: Maskandi, Kwaito, Amapiano, Isicathamiya, Mbaqanga and Gqom among them.

The first result is that classifier-free guidance, rather than the language adaptation itself, controls whether the model sings the requested isiZulu words. The second is less comfortable. The caption-retrieval control I built to validate the genre metric turned out to be broken in three independent ways. Each one pushed towards a false negative, and not one of them raised an error. Corrected, the correct caption ranks 6.6 places above a mismatched one.

Intent detection, query rephrasing, Redis KNN retrieval, cross-encoder re-ranking, generation with source citations. PII is masked before any chunk reaches the model, tenants are isolated by JWT scope, and images pass through a vision model into searchable text.

SIGMA, a human-in-the-loop co-agent

Independent
2025

The model proposes structured diffs to a document and a person accepts or rejects each one. Propose, apply, undo, redo, version history, idempotency by proposal hashing, full audit trail. An autonomy toggle lets the operator decide how much latitude the agent gets, which makes it a small concrete study of where the human belongs in an agent loop.
